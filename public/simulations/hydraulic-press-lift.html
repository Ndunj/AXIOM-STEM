Physics Principles of the Hydraulic Press

1. Pascal's Principle

A hydraulic press relies on Pascal's Principle, which states that any change in pressure applied to an enclosed, incompressible fluid is transmitted undiminished throughout the entire fluid.

P₁ = P₂ = P

2. Force Multiplication

Because pressure is equal to force divided by cross-sectional area, a small force applied on a smaller piston ($A_1$) creates fluid pressure that produces a much larger output force on a larger piston ($A_2$).

F₂ = P × A₂ = F₁ × (A₂ / A₁)

3. Conservation of Work & Energy

Force is multiplied at the expense of distance. Work input equals work output ($W = F \times d$). The input piston must move a much greater distance ($d_1$) than the output piston moves ($d_2$).

F₁ × d₁ = F₂ × d₂
