Physics Note: Blackbody Radiation Phenomenon

1. What is a Blackbody?

An idealized physical body that absorbs all incident electromagnetic radiation, regardless of frequency or angle of incidence. When held at a uniform thermal equilibrium temperature, it emits radiation according to Planck's Law.

2. Planck's Radiation Law

Proposed by Max Planck in 1900, it accurately describes the spectral radiance emitted by a blackbody across all wavelengths by introducing quantized energy levels:

I(λ, T) = (2 × h × c²) / [ λ⁵ × (e(h × c / (λ × kB × T)) - 1) ]

3. Wien's Displacement Law

States that the peak emission wavelength is inversely proportional to the absolute temperature. As temperature rises, the peak shifts toward shorter (bluer) wavelengths:

λmax = b / T (where b ≈ 2.8977 × 10⁻³ m × K)

4. Stefan-Boltzmann Law

The total radiant energy per unit surface area integrated across all wavelengths is directly proportional to the fourth power of absolute temperature:

P / A = σ × T⁴ (where σ ≈ 5.670374 × 10⁻⁸ W / (m² × K⁴))

5. The Ultraviolet Catastrophe

Classical physics predicted via the Rayleigh-Jeans law that an ideal blackbody would emit infinite power at infinitely short wavelengths (ultraviolet range). Planck's energy quantization assumption resolved this paradox, launching modern Quantum Mechanics.
