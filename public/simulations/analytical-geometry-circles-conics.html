Formula Guide & Lab Instructions

Mode A: Circles & Tangent Lines

A circle is defined by the standard Cartesian equation: (x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2, where (h, k) is the center and r is the radius.

  • Radius Vector: Connects the center (h, k) to the point of tangency on the circle.
  • Tangent Line: A line touching the circle at exactly one point. It is strictly perpendicular to the radius vector at the point of contact (slope product m1 * m2 = -1).
  • External Point Tangents: When point P is outside the circle, exactly two distinct tangent lines can be drawn from P to the circle.

Mode B: Conic Sections

Conic sections are curves formed by intersecting a plane with a double right circular cone:

  • Ellipse: ((x - h)^2 / a^2) + ((y - k)^2 / b^2) = 1. The sum of distances from any point on the ellipse to the two foci is constant (2a or 2b). Eccentricity e = sqrt(1 - b^2/a^2).
  • Parabola: (x - h)^2 = 4p(y - k). The set of all points equidistant from a fixed point (focus) and a fixed line (directrix).
  • Hyperbola: ((x - h)^2 / a^2) - ((y - k)^2 / b^2) = 1. The absolute difference of distances from any point on the hyperbola to the two foci is constant (2a). Asymptotes pass through the center with slopes +/- (b/a).
