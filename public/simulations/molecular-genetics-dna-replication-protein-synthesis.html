Explanatory Lesson Notes

1. DNA Structure & Base Pairing

DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid) is composed of two anti-parallel strands forming a double helix. The nitrogenous bases pair specifically via hydrogen bonding:

  • Adenine (A) pairs with Thymine (T) (2 hydrogen bonds).
  • Cytosine (C) pairs with Guanine (G) (3 hydrogen bonds).

2. DNA Replication

Prior to cell division, DNA replicates in a semi-conservative manner where each new double helix contains one original and one synthesized strand.

  • DNA Helicase: Unzips the double helix by breaking weak hydrogen bonds between complementary base pairs.
  • Replication Fork: The Y-shaped region where DNA unzips into leading and lagging strands.
  • DNA Polymerase: Synthesizes complementary daughter strands from 5' to 3'.

3. Transcription (DNA to mRNA)

Transcription takes place in the nucleus where genetic instructions are copied from DNA into messenger RNA (mRNA).

  • RNA Polymerase: Unwinds DNA and adds RNA nucleotides along the template strand.
  • Uracil Sub-Rule: In RNA, Uracil (U) replaces Thymine (T) to pair with Adenine (A).

4. Translation (mRNA to Protein)

Translation takes place at the ribosome in the cytoplasm, converting the mRNA sequence into a polypeptide chain of amino acids.

  • Codons: 3-nucleotide sequences on mRNA (e.g., AUG = Methionine/Start).
  • Anticodons: Complementary 3-nucleotide sequences carried by tRNA molecules.
  • Ribosome: Facilitates matching between codons and anticodons, linking amino acids via peptide bonds.
