In 1926, Austrian mathematician Karl Menger introduced this 3D fractal construction while attempting to formulate a rigorous topological definition of dimension.

Building upon Wacław Sierpiński's earlier work on 2D carpet fractals, Menger proved that his 3D sponge was a universal curve: any topological curve of dimension 1 can be embedded inside a Menger Sponge structure without self-intersection.

The Menger Sponge exhibits a famous geometric paradox as iteration level L approaches infinity:

lim (L → &infty;) Volume = (20/27)^L = 0

Its fractional topological scale is governed by the Hausdorff Dimension formula:

D_H = log(20) / log(3) ≈ 2.7268

This places the sponge strictly between a 2D surface (Dimension = 2) and a 3D solid (Dimension = 3).
