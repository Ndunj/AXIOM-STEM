How the Double Pendulum Works

A double pendulum consists of a primary physical pendulum with a secondary pendulum attached to its bob. Despite being governed by fully deterministic classical mechanics (Newton's laws), its physical motion is famous for displaying extreme deterministic chaos.

1. Non-Linear Coupling

Unlike a simple pendulum whose motion depends only on its own restoring force, the upper bob (m1) serves as a moving pivot for the lower bob (m2). The acceleration of m1 creates fictitious inertial forces on m2, while the swinging of m2 exerts dynamic forces back onto m1.

2. Energy Exchange & Whipping

Potential energy continually transforms into kinetic energy and back again. When energy concentrates into the outer rod (l2), it executes rapid "whipping" movements or flips full 360° loops over the central pivot.

3. Sensitive Initial Conditions

The system exhibits the Butterfly Effect. Releasing the top rod from an angle difference as tiny as 0.0001° yields wildly different trajectory paths within just a few swings, making long-term predictions impossible without continuous ultra-high-precision integration.

Guide to Interactive Controls

Gravity (g)

Effect: Controls the downward acceleration restoring force.
• High Gravity: Accelerates motion, increases kinetic energy transfers, and causes rapid whipping.
• Zero Gravity (0 m/s²): Pendulum drifts linearly across its pivot due to conservation of angular momentum without gravitational pull.

Mass Parameters (m1 vs m2)

Effect: Alters the inertia ratio and cross-coupling momentum.
• Heavy m1, Light m2: The top arm acts as a dominant driver; the bottom arm behaves like a chaotic tail.
• Light m1, Heavy m2: The bottom mass heavily destabilizes and jerks the primary pivot arm.

Arm Lengths (l1 vs l2)

Effect: Changes torque leverage and moment of inertia.
• Longer arms increase spatial sweeps and visual canvas radius.
• Shortening an arm decreases its moment of inertia, forcing it to rotate faster to conserve energy.

Trail Length & System Kick

• Trail Length: Sets how many previous coordinate points of the outer bob (m2) are retained to render the visual trajectory path.
• Perturb System (Kick): Imparts a sudden velocity nudge to the upper joint, demonstrating how small force inputs drastically transform chaotic paths.
