📚 High School Genetics Laboratory Reference Guide

1. Mendelian Inheritance Principles

• Law of Segregation: Alleles segregate during gamete formation so that each gamete carries only one allele per gene.
• Law of Independent Assortment: Genes for different traits assort independently during gamete formation (valid for unlinked genes).

2. Non-Mendelian Patterns

• Incomplete Dominance: Heterozygotes display an intermediate, blended phenotype (e.g., Red $RR$ x White $WW$ = Pink $RW$).
• Codominance: Both alleles are fully expressed simultaneously without blending (e.g., ABO Blood Group $I^A I^B$ = Type AB).
• Sex-Linked Traits: Genes located on sex chromosomes (X chromosome). Males ($XY$) are hemizygous and express recessive traits more frequently than females ($XX$).

3. Pedigree Deduction Guidelines

• Autosomal Recessive: Trait can skip generations; affected offspring can be born to unaffected heterozygous parents.
• Autosomal Dominant: Trait usually appears in every generation; affected individuals must have at least one affected parent.
• X-Linked Recessive: Significantly higher incidence in males; affected mothers pass the allele to all sons.
