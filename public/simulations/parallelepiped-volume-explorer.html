Mathematical & Geometric Explanatory Note

Deep dive into the vector operations governing the simulation.

1. Geometric Meaning of the Cross Product (b × c)

Vectors b and c form the base parallelogram of the parallelepiped. Their cross product yields a normal vector N = b × c whose length equals the area of this base parallelogram: A = |b × c|.

2. Scalar Triple Product and Volume

The volume V of a parallelepiped is the product of its base area and its perpendicular height (h). Since height is the scalar projection of vector a onto the normal direction of the base, taking the absolute value of the dot product between a and N gives the total volume: V = |a · (b × c)|.

3. The 3x3 Determinant Connection

The scalar triple product is identically equal to the determinant of the 3x3 matrix formed by placing vectors a, b, and c as its rows. If this determinant evaluates to zero, the vectors are linearly dependent and lie in a single common plane (coplanar degenerate state).
