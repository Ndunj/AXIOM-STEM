Black Hole Science & Features Guide

General Relativity, Relativistic Horizons & Simulator Controls

The Ultimate Gravitational Trap

A black hole is a region of spacetime where gravity is so strong that nothing—not even light—has enough energy to escape its gravitational pull. Albert Einstein's Theory of General Relativity predicts that a sufficiently compact mass can deform spacetime to form a black hole.

Schwarzschild Black Holes

Non-rotating (static) black holes characterized solely by mass ($M$). The metric geometry is spherically symmetric, with a spherical event horizon at radius $r_s = \frac{2GM}{c^2}$.

Kerr Rotating Black Holes

Rotating black holes with angular momentum ($a$). Rotation drags space around itself (frame dragging) and flattens the outer region into an oblate Ergosphere.

Event Horizon ($r_s$)

The boundary beyond which escape velocity exceeds the speed of light. Anything crossing this radius is irrevocably drawn into the central singularity.

Ergosphere ($r_{ergo}$)

An oblate region outside the event horizon of rotating Kerr black holes. Spacetime is dragged so fast that objects cannot stand still (Frame Dragging / Penrose Process).

Photon Sphere ($1.5 r_s$)

A spherical boundary where gravity is strong enough that light photons are forced to orbit in circular trajectories around the black hole.

ISCO Ring ($3.0 r_s$)

Innermost Stable Circular Orbit. The innermost boundary where matter can orbit safely without spiraling into the horizon.

Gravitational Time Dilation

According to General Relativity, time runs slower near massive gravitational objects. As an infalling observer approaches $r_s$, time stops relative to a far observer ($d\tau = dt \cdot \sqrt{1 - r_s / r}$).
