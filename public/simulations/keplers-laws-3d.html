Kepler's Second Law

"A line segment joining a planet and the Sun sweeps out equal areas during equal intervals of time."
Explanation: Because the orbit is elliptical, the planet speeds up near perihelion (closest approach) and slows down near aphelion (farthest approach) to keep the swept area constant.

Kepler's Third Law

"The square of the orbital period of a planet is directly proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis of its orbit (T² ∝ a³)."
Explanation: Larger orbits require exponentially longer times to complete a full revolution. The ratio T²/a³ remains constant for all bodies orbiting the Sun.
