Chemistry Concept Guide

Endpoint vs. Equivalence Point:
• Equivalence Point: The theoretical point where moles of hydrogen ions equal moles of hydroxide ions, marking 100% complete chemical neutralization.
• Endpoint: The physical observation point where the indicator visually changes color.
• Experimental Rule: Turn OFF the buret pipe at the ENDPOINT (first permanent color change). In actual practice, you cannot see the equivalence point directly; you rely on the indicator's endpoint to signal that neutralization is complete.

Expected pH at Complete Neutralization (Equivalence Point)

The pH at neutralization depends on the strengths of the reacting acid and base, as salt hydrolysis affects the final ion concentration:

System Type pH at Neutralization Reasoning
Strong Acid + Strong Base pH = 7.00 Salt ions do not undergo hydrolysis in water.
Weak Acid + Strong Base pH > 7.00 (Basic) Conjugate base of weak acid reacts with water to produce excess OH- ions.
Strong Acid + Weak Base pH < 7.00 (Acidic) Conjugate acid of weak base reacts with water to produce excess H+ ions.

Choosing the Right Indicator

An indicator is accurate only when its pH color transition range encompasses the equivalence point of the specific reaction system.
